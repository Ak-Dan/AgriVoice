## Symptoms

Cassava brown streak disease can affect both leaves and storage roots. Leaf symptoms may be subtle, but infected storage roots can develop brown, corky necrotic areas that make roots unsuitable for food or sale.

## Why It Matters

CBSD is an important constraint in cassava-growing areas of East, Central, and Southern Africa. It reduces plant growth and root quality and can spread through infected planting material and whitefly vectors.

## Farmer-Safe Guidance

- Do not use cuttings from plants that show suspicious leaf symptoms, stem streaking, or root necrosis.
- Plant clean, disease-free cassava stems from a trusted source.
- Remove and destroy heavily symptomatic plants when advised by extension workers.
- Avoid moving cassava planting materials from affected areas to new farms.
- Ask an extension worker to confirm suspected CBSD, especially before sharing or selling planting stems.

## Prevention

Use resistant or tolerant varieties where available, maintain clean seed systems, and monitor fields regularly. Community-level management is important because infected cuttings and whiteflies can spread disease between farms.

## Escalation

Escalate when several plants show symptoms, roots show brown necrosis, or disease appears in a new community.
